Cash-Flow Forecasting for a Small Business

Business question: "What projections can we expect from our business in the next 5 years? We want to expand, but need to know if our current sales model is working."

A fictional stakeholder scenario built on a real analytical workflow: forecasting 5-year cash flow for a 3-location Calgary bookstore chain using 5 years of financial history, and answering whether current performance supports expansion.

TL;DR

Current cash flow trends do not support expansion. The business is projected to run a persistent ~$27K–28K monthly cash deficit through 2025 and beyond, totaling an estimated -$1.7M cumulative cash flow over 5 years (plausible range: -$380K to -$3.0M). The deficit is structural (i.e., consistent across months rather than driven by any single swinging cost or revenue line), meaning the fix isn't "one bad quarter to manage," but a deeper look at fixed costs vs. revenue at the unit-economics level.

Key result

Historical net cash flow (2019–2024) and 5-year SARIMA forecast with 95% confidence interval. The shaded band widens over time, reflecting appropriately growing uncertainty further into the forecast.

What this project does

  • Cleans and reconciles 6 years of daily/biweekly/quarterly financial data (checking, credit, sales, payroll, loans) into a single monthly cash-flow view
  • Diagnoses whether the current sales model is financially sustainable, using correlation analysis against revenue, payroll, and loan repayment
  • Forecasts 5-year cash flow using three methods of increasing sophistication (naive seasonal baseline, linear regression, and SARIMA) each validated on a 12-month holdout period before being trusted
  • Selects a final model based on forecast accuracy (MAE), not just in-sample fit, and is explicit in the write-up about where a simpler model actually outperformed a more complex one
  • Delivers findings as a stakeholder-ready executive summary with a clear recommendation, confidence ranges, and disclosed limitations, not just a chart

Why this project (a note on approach)

A meaningful part of this project was the debugging and validation process itself — catching a sign error in how credit card liability was combined with checking balance, catching an over-differenced SARIMA configuration that was quietly hurting accuracy, and being willing to report that a simple regression underperformed a naive baseline rather than hiding it. The full notebooks preserve this process; the write-up reflects only validated, sanity-checked findings.

Tech stack

  • Python — pandas, numpy
  • Forecasting — statsmodels (SARIMAX), scikit-learn (LinearRegression)
  • Visualization — matplotlib
